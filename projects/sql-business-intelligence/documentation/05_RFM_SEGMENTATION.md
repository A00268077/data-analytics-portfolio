# RFM Customer Segmentation Module

## Overview

This module performs customer segmentation using the RFM methodology in PostgreSQL.

RFM evaluates customer behaviour using three dimensions:

- **Recency** — how recently a customer completed a purchase
- **Frequency** — how many completed orders the customer placed
- **Monetary** — how much realised revenue the customer generated

The purpose of the analysis is to transform transactional customer history into actionable behavioural segments that can support retention, loyalty, reactivation, and customer-value strategies.

The analytical reference date is **2025-12-31**, matching the end of the portfolio dataset.

---

## Business Objectives

The RFM module answers the following questions:

- Who are the company's strongest customer relationships?
- Which customers purchased most recently?
- Which customers purchase most frequently?
- Which customers generate the highest lifetime revenue?
- Which customers qualify as Champions?
- Which customers are Loyal Customers?
- Which customers could become loyal customers?
- Which historically valuable customers are now at risk?
- Which customers appear lost or inactive?
- How large is each RFM customer segment?
- How much revenue does each RFM segment generate?
- Which RFM segments have the highest average order value?
- Where are high-value customer segments geographically concentrated?
- How do RFM segments differ between Consumer, Small Business, and Corporate customers?
- Which customers should be prioritised for retention action?
- How can RFM segmentation be used in Power BI?

## RFM Methodology

### Recency

Recency measures the number of days between the analytical reference date and the customer's most recent completed order.

```text
Recency = 2025-12-31 - Last Completed Order Date
```

Lower recency values indicate more recent customer activity.

Customers are divided into five groups using `NTILE(5)`.

The most recent customers receive the highest Recency score.

---

### Frequency

Frequency measures the number of distinct completed orders generated by each customer.

```text
Frequency = Number of Completed Orders
```

Customers with higher purchasing frequency receive higher scores.

---

### Monetary

Monetary value represents the total realised revenue generated by each customer.

```text
Monetary = Sum of Completed-Order Revenue
```

Customers generating greater lifetime revenue receive higher Monetary scores.

---

## RFM Scoring

Each dimension receives a score from 1 to 5:

```text
1 = Lowest
5 = Highest
```

The individual scores are combined into an RFM code.

Example:

```text
R = 5
F = 5
M = 5

RFM Score = 555
```

A total score is also calculated:

```text
RFM Total Score = R + F + M
```

The maximum possible total is 15.

## RFM Segments

The model converts numerical RFM scores into business-friendly customer segments.

### Champions

Customers with strong Recency, Frequency, and Monetary scores.

These are the company's most valuable relationships.

Recommended strategy:

- Protect the relationship
- Reward loyalty
- Provide premium service
- Offer early access or VIP benefits

---

### Loyal Customers

Customers who purchase frequently and remain reasonably recent.

Recommended strategy:

- Retention programmes
- Cross-sell
- Upsell
- Loyalty benefits

---

### Potential Loyalists

Recent customers showing encouraging repeat-purchase behaviour.

Recommended strategy:

- Encourage additional purchases
- Personalised product recommendations
- Loyalty-programme onboarding

---

### New Customers

Very recent customers with limited purchase history.

Recommended strategy:

- Strong onboarding
- Follow-up communication
- Second-purchase incentives

---

### Promising

Relatively recent customers with limited purchasing frequency.

Recommended strategy:

- Targeted recommendations
- Engagement campaigns
- Purchase-frequency incentives

---

### Needs Attention

Customers whose engagement is weakening.

Recommended strategy:

- Personalised re-engagement
- Product recommendations
- Time-limited incentives

---

### At Risk

Historically frequent or valuable customers whose recent activity has declined.

This is one of the most strategically important RFM segments.

Recommended strategy:

- Priority retention campaign
- Personal account outreach
- Targeted win-back offer
- Investigate reasons for inactivity

---

### Hibernating

Customers with weak recent activity and moderate historical purchasing behaviour.

Recommended strategy:

- Selective reactivation
- Automated win-back campaigns
- Low-cost promotional outreach

---

### Lost

Customers with both poor Recency and Frequency scores.

Recommended strategy:

- Low-priority reactivation
- Automated campaigns
- Avoid excessive acquisition spending

---

## Segment Size Analysis

The module measures how many customers belong to each RFM segment and calculates customer-share percentages.

This helps management understand the behavioural composition of the customer base.

For example, a business with a large At Risk population may require a stronger retention strategy, while a large Champion population indicates strong customer loyalty.

---

## Revenue by RFM Segment

Revenue is aggregated by behavioural segment.

The analysis includes:

- Number of customers
- Segment revenue
- Average customer value
- Revenue share %

This allows management to compare customer population size with financial contribution.

A small RFM segment may still be strategically important if it generates a disproportionately large share of revenue.

---

## Average Order Value

Average order value is compared across RFM segments.

This identifies whether segments differ because of:

- Purchase frequency
- Transaction size
- Both factors

For example, Champions may generate value through both frequent purchases and high order values.

---

## Geographic RFM Analysis

RFM segments are compared across countries.

This helps identify geographic concentrations of:

- Champions
- Loyal Customers
- At Risk customers
- Lost customers

Geographic RFM analysis can support country-specific retention and marketing strategies.

---

## Customer-Type RFM Analysis

RFM performance is also compared across:

- Consumer
- Small Business
- Corporate

This helps determine whether different customer types display different loyalty and purchasing patterns.

---

## High-Value Customers at Risk

The module specifically identifies customers with weak Recency scores but strong historical Frequency or Monetary scores.

These customers are important because they have previously demonstrated significant business value but are becoming inactive.

They should generally receive higher retention priority than low-value inactive customers.

---

## Champions Analysis

A dedicated query produces a list of Champion customers.

These customers demonstrate:

- Recent activity
- High purchasing frequency
- High lifetime value

They represent the strongest relationships in the customer portfolio.

---

## Lost Customer Analysis

Lost customers are identified using the weakest Recency and Frequency scores.

Historical revenue is retained in the output so management can distinguish between:

- Low-value lost customers
- Previously valuable customers who may justify reactivation investment

---

## RFM Action Framework

The module maps each RFM segment to a recommended commercial action.

Examples include:

- Protect and reward Champions
- Retain Loyal Customers
- Develop Potential Loyalists
- Prevent churn among At Risk customers
- Selectively reactivate Hibernating customers
- Use low-cost win-back strategies for Lost customers

This converts SQL segmentation into an actionable business framework.
